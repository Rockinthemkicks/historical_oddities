## 3. Final script

Claim ids in brackets are for review only and aren't read aloud. Lines starting 'It lands as...', 'In Avery's plan...' or 'The evidence points...' are labelled interpretations, backed by interpretation claims.

**1. The Man Who Tried to Leave** (135 words)

Two years before Tommy Vercetti took over Vice City, another man was trying to escape it. <sub>[C08, C32, C22]</sub> His name was Victor Vance. <sub>[C10]</sub> A soldier turned drug dealer, Vic had survived a corrupt sergeant, a war with a powerful cartel, and the loss of the woman he loved. <sub>[C10, C11, C15, C16, C50]</sub> By the end of 1984, he was ready to leave it all behind. <sub>[C32, C47]</sub> Two years later, he was back, at a drug deal that would end his life and begin Tommy Vercetti's rise to power. <sub>[C32, C20, C48]</sub> Vice City tells the story of Tommy building a criminal empire. <sub>[C48]</sub> But the empire was already there. <sub>[C22, C43]</sub> Behind the neon and the oceanfront mansions, a corrupt sergeant, rival drug lords and an undercover agent working both sides had been shaping the city since at least 1984. <sub>[C11, C13, C14, C39, C49]</sub> Tommy didn't invent the system. <sub>[C43]</sub> He inherited it. <sub>[C43]</sub>

**2. Which Vice City?** (62 words)

First, which Vice City is this? <sub>[C51, C52]</sub> Vice City Stories, set in 1984, and Vice City, set in 1986, share one continuity: the series' 3D era. <sub>[C08, C51]</sub> Grand Theft Auto VI's modern Vice City, in the fictional state of Leonida, belongs to a separate continuity. <sub>[C05, C52]</sub> This is the story of the older city. It isn't GTA VI's backstory, and it won't spoil GTA VI. <sub>[C51, C52]</sub>

**3. The Sergeant** (119 words)

In 1984, Victor Vance is an Army corporal at Fort Baxter, on the edge of Vice City. <sub>[C08, C10]</sub> He needs money for his brother Pete's asthma medication. <sub>[C31]</sub> His sergeant, Jerry Martinez, runs a secret drug business, and Vic is drawn in as a reluctant middleman. <sub>[C11]</sub> Then Martinez frames him. Drugs are found, there's a sex worker in Vic's car, and Vic is dishonorably discharged. <sub>[C11]</sub> Vic later learns whose cocaine Martinez had been moving: the Mendez brothers', the most powerful drug family in the city. <sub>[C12, C13]</sub> An Army sergeant, working as a courier for a cartel. <sub>[C12]</sub> Vic, meanwhile, keeps saying he wants out of the drug game. When Lance offers him twenty kilos of cocaine to get started, he turns it down. <sub>[C47]</sub>

**4. The Reluctant Empire** (103 words)

Vic's way out of the Army leads straight into the rackets. <sub>[C53, C11]</sub> He goes to work for Marty Jay Williams, the leader of a street gang called the Trailer Park Mafia. <sub>[C53]</sub> When Marty is killed, Louise suggests that Vic take over Marty's businesses. He does, and the gang becomes the Vance Crime Family. <sub>[C53, C50]</sub> Its businesses read like a map of the city's underground economy: protection, loan sharking, prostitution, drugs, smuggling and robbery. <sub>[C54]</sub> So the reluctant soldier closes out 1984 as the head of a crime family, side by side with his brother. In this city, even the exit seems to run through the rackets. <sub>[C55, C53]</sub>

**5. The Agent** (99 words)

Then there's Bryan Forbes, an undercover agent who sells the Vance brothers information about drug deals. <sub>[C39]</sub> Forbes's own ID becomes part of the scheme. In The Mugshot Longshot, Lance pastes Martinez's photo onto it, to convince the Mendez brothers that their sergeant is working for the law. <sub>[C41]</sub> An agent selling tips to dealers. A sergeant running cocaine for a cartel. <sub>[C39, C12]</sub> Neither proves the whole Army or the whole police force was corrupt, and the games never say so. <sub>[C42]</sub> But in these cases, the men trusted to stand between Vice City and its drug trade were already part of it. <sub>[C42]</sub>

**6. The Cartel's Tower** (191 words)

The Mendez brothers, Armando and Diego, run the 1984 drug trade from a downtown tower also known as the Mendez Foundation building. <sub>[C13]</sub> A charitable-sounding name on a cartel's headquarters. A small detail, and a telling one. <sub>[C13]</sub> The brothers use the Vances for a few jobs, then betray them, and Vic goes to war with the cartel. <sub>[C15]</sub> It costs him dearly. Louise, the woman he loves, dies after the Mendez brothers kidnap her, and Armando is killed at the Mendez mansion. <sub>[C16, C50]</sub> Vic's last backer is a rival drug baron, Ricardo Diaz, who gives him work against the Mendez operation. <sub>[C14]</sub> Diaz is already maneuvering. That same year he blackmails Gonzalez, the right-hand man of a retired colonel, for information on the colonel's drug shipments, as part of his plan to become the city's drug baron. <sub>[C49, C34]</sub> The final mission, Last Stand, is a job for Diaz. Vic attacks the Mendez building from the air and kills Diego Mendez and Martinez on the roof. <sub>[C38, C14]</sub> Then Vic and Lance decide to leave town and lay low. <sub>[C32]</sub> Lance suggests hiding the leftover cocaine and selling it later. Vic's answer is flat: he is not interested. <sub>[C65]</sub>

**7. The Exchange** (184 words)

Two years later, Vic is back at a deal table. The games never show how he returned. <sub>[C32]</sub> On the other side of that table is Tommy Vercetti. Sonny Forelli has sent him south to keep him out of Liberty City and to get the family a cut of the drug money. <sub>[C19, C63]</sub> In the opening scene, he says it plainly: Vice City is twenty-four carat gold these days. <sub>[C63]</sub> The first person Tommy meets in Vice City isn't a gangster. It's a lawyer. <sub>[C56]</sub> Ken Rosenberg, the Forelli family's lawyer, picks Tommy up at the airport and drives him straight to the deal. <sub>[C56]</sub> According to Rosenberg, the deal was set up with help from a retired colonel, Juan Garcia Cortez. <sub>[C33]</sub> The deal is ambushed. Vic and two Forelli men are killed, the money and the cocaine are taken, and Lance escapes. <sub>[C20]</sub> Cortez later tells Tommy who talked: his own right-hand man, Gonzalez, gave away information about the deal. Tommy kills him in Treacherous Swine. <sub>[C34]</sub> The game never says who Gonzalez talked to. But two years earlier, Diaz had blackmailed him for information on Cortez's shipments. <sub>[C34, C49]</sub>

**8. The Heir** (79 words)

Tommy goes to work for Diaz. Lance, working alongside him, learns that Diaz's gang carried out the ambush. <sub>[C21]</sub> Together they storm Diaz's mansion on Starfish Island and kill him, and Tommy takes the mansion and Diaz's empire. <sub>[C22]</sub> From there, Tommy rises to become Vice City's major criminal power. <sub>[C48]</sub> But the mansion and the drug empire he builds on belonged to Diaz first. <sub>[C22, C43]</sub> Lance stays at his side, but he starts to feel underappreciated in a rise he helped begin. <sub>[C25]</sub>

**9. The Business of Vice** (314 words)

Drugs weren't the only business in Tommy's Vice City. <sub>[C46, C45]</sub> Avery Carrington is a real-estate developer who gives Tommy work in a set of optional side missions. <sub>[C46]</sub> In Four Iron, a rival developer won't sell Avery some land, and Tommy kills him on a golf course. <sub>[C46]</sub> In Demolition Man, a competing construction site is in Avery's way, and Tommy blows it up. <sub>[C46]</sub> In Two Bit Hit, Avery puts it plainly: nothing brings down real estate prices quicker than a good old-fashioned gang war. <sub>[C36]</sub> Tommy dresses as a Cuban gang member and attacks a Haitian funeral, so the two gangs go to war. <sub>[C35]</sub> In Avery's plan, violence is simply a way to move the market. <sub>[C36]</sub> Taking notes through all of this is Avery's young protégé, Donald Love, who writes down the gang-war tactic in Two Bit Hit. <sub>[C59]</sub> Years later, in Grand Theft Auto III, Love is a real-estate developer who owns a media company. Avery's playbook didn't stay in Vice City. <sub>[C60, C62]</sub> Tommy's own businesses run on similar logic. At the Print Works, the foreman suggests printing money, and Tommy steals a courier's printing plates to start counterfeiting. <sub>[C45]</sub> Then there's the bank. From his new nightclub, the Malibu, Tommy plans a robbery of El Banco Corrupto Grande, a name that translates as the big corrupt bank. <sub>[C58]</sub> Introducing his crew, Tommy jokes that Ken, the lawyer, washes the money for them. <sub>[C57]</sub> Even the bank wears its corruption on its sign. It's the game's own satire, and it's hard to miss. <sub>[C61]</sub> And when Congressman Alex Shrub moves to ban pornography to win puritan votes, Tommy, who now owns a film studio, gets incriminating photos of the congressman to stop him. <sub>[C44]</sub> A developer who uses murder to win land. A business that prints its own money. A congressman kept in line with photographs. <sub>[C46, C45, C44]</sub> By 1986, Tommy isn't outside Vice City's system. He's fluent in it. <sub>[C48, C43]</sub>

**10. This Is Business** (81 words)

The counterfeit money has one destination: Sonny Forelli, who comes south to collect. <sub>[C23, C45]</sub> Sonny arrives joking about Tommy's old nickname: the Harwood Butcher. <sub>[C18, C24]</sub> Tommy says he was sent to kill one man, and they knew he was coming. Sonny's answer: those fifteen years in prison were his to spend. <sub>[C24]</sub> It lands as a confession. <sub>[C64]</sub> Lance has already switched sides. He tells Sonny about the fake money, and Tommy kills them both. <sub>[C23]</sub> Lance's explanation for the betrayal is short: this is business. <sub>[C23, C25]</sub>

**11. Built on Crime** (102 words)

Two men, two years apart. <sub>[C08]</sub> Vic Vance said he wanted out, and the city brought him back to a deal table where he died. <sub>[C47, C32, C20]</sub> Tommy Vercetti wanted the top, and he got it. <sub>[C48]</sub> The difference is that Vic kept looking for the exit, and Tommy went looking for the keys. <sub>[C47, C48]</sub> Vic built an empire because the city left him little else. Rosenberg washed Tommy's money. Avery's protégé carried the playbook to another city. <sub>[C55, C57, C62]</sub> The sergeant, the agent, the cartel and the colonel were all there before Tommy arrived. <sub>[C42, C13, C49]</sub> The evidence points one way. Tommy didn't corrupt Vice City. He inherited it. <sub>[C43]</sub>
